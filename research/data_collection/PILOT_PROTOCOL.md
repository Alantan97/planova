# Collection pilot and data checks

Draft v0.1 - 18 October 2026. Feasibility protocol to review with the supervisor, not an approved final research design.

## Before distribution

1. Confirm the official names of the three UPSI programmes and who is eligible.
2. Complete participant information, contact/storage/retention/withdrawal arrangements, and any required university review.
3. Confirm that the ranking target is students' stated preferences. Do not describe it as an objectively optimal study order.
4. Walk through the questionnaire and snapshot template with the researcher, using a multi-task example with exact dates and explicit effort units.
5. Check the language version; pilot translations consistently if Malay is offered.

## Small pilot

An initial practical suggestion is 5–8 participants and two snapshots each, if feasible. Include students across the three intended programmes where recruitment permits and record which programmes are absent. This tests the instrument and logistics; it does not establish enough training data or a representative final sample.

Collect the first snapshot and repeat at an agreed later planning moment, for example 3–7 days later. Record actual timestamps, including unchanged tasks and priorities where reported. Do not tell students that priorities must change.

Observe whether students understand remaining effort, exact deadlines, difficulty versus importance, and priority versus assessment weight. Record completion time, clarification requests, missing fields, invalid ranks, and willingness to return. Ask a short debrief: Which question was confusing? Did any important commitment not fit? Could you express equal priorities?

Do not impose a target number of task rows by inventing tasks or duplicating records. An informative learning-to-rank collection needs varied planning situations and meaningful preference comparisons, not simply a large CSV.

## Data handling and structure

Keep unchanged source responses, a cleaned working copy, and a dated cleaning log. Suggested tables are participants/preferences, snapshots, snapshot_tasks, availability_blocks, and document_reference. Contact information, if needed for recruitment, is in a separate file with a participant-code lookup, not in model inputs.

For a repeat observation, the participant ID stays the same; the snapshot ID changes; persistent tasks keep their task IDs. This linkage means participant-coded/pseudonymous data, not an unconditional anonymity promise.

## Checks before accepting an observation

- Participation agreed and eligibility met.
- Participant code is valid; snapshot ID is unique; snapshot time includes date and time zone.
- Remaining effort has a declared unit and is converted to minutes only when known.
- A deadline stated only as Friday or Week 5 remains unresolved until confirmed.
- Known assessment weights fall between 0 and 100; unknown is not coded as zero.
- Ranks are positive integers, ties are retained, and participant omissions are visible.
- Distinguish truly complete ranking groups from groups with unable-to-judge tasks.
- List positions, labels, feature values, instrument version, and source provenance are retained.
- No task rows have been manufactured from a proforma's total SLT as if they were participant-reported effort.

Ask for clarification where permitted; otherwise preserve uncertainty. Keep the original and correction history. Exclusion rules must be documented before final model comparison.

## Pilot review and expansion

Review question clarity and data usability with the supervisor before choosing final recruitment size or launching a larger collection. Assess number and diversity of informative groups, missingness, label variation, programme coverage, repeated tasks, and available evaluation participants. Do not promise a reliable model based only on the pilot size.

For evaluating new-student performance, keep participants separate across training/validation/test. For later-time prediction within students, use a justified chronological design with controls for overlapping/repeated tasks. Make the intended generalization claim explicit. Reserve final evaluation before tuning.

Evaluate ranking against held-out student judgments, not against labels generated by the same baseline formula. Different students disagreeing is not automatically an annotation mistake; their personal preferences are the target.

## What the pilot does not test

- Whether following student preferences improves grades.
- Whether a proforma extraction pipeline is accurate without manually verified document fields.
- Whether a GA finds good schedules without specified feasibility rules and comparison cases.
- Whether all three programmes are covered merely because the questionnaire lists them.

## Next steps after pilot

Revise wording, freeze a version, decide recruitment/evaluation with the supervisor, then collect the expanded data. Continue document-extraction development and baseline scheduling independently of model training.
