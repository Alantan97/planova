# Google Form draft review

Reviewed: 20 October 2026.
Source: `research/data_collection/Planova_ Study Planning Needs and Preferences of UPSI Students - Google Forms.pdf`.
Scope: text from all nine PDF pages, a rendered overview of all pages, and detailed visual checks of pages 1, 4, 5, and 9. No online form was edited or submitted.

## Overall assessment

The draft includes all 21 planned questions and addresses the target UPSI programmes, course-proforma use, planning difficulties, study preferences, personal commitments, protected time, and adjustment control. The optional follow-up is clearly separate from the first questionnaire. It is close to a small instrument pilot after the corrections and setup checks below.

This questionnaire collects requirements and stated preferences. It does not yet provide the task sets and student-chosen priority labels required for the proposed ranking experiment.

- I am not sure what a course proforma is.
- I do not usually access one.
- Other [built-in text option].

> Select all that apply. If you choose "I would not want to use document import" or "Not sure", select that option only.

Q12/Q13 description, optional improvement:

> If you select Other, enter a duration in minutes.

Q14 description, optional addition:

> If you select Other, enter your preferred daily maximum in minutes.

The main introduction can keep its existing wording; add a short real contact/data-handling block rather than rewriting the entire form. Those details must come from the researcher/supervisor rather than being invented in this review.

## What appears appropriate

- All 21 questions are present in the PDF; checkbox versus single-choice controls generally match the questions.
- Q10 and Q18 appear optional, keeping personal explanations non-mandatory.
- The form does not ask for matric numbers, medical details, or other people's names.
- Q11 distinguishes preferences from permission to schedule over sleep or commitments.
- Q12–Q14 use explicit minute-based choices and allow variation.
- Q15–Q18 cover non-academic commitments without demanding private circumstances.
- Q19 allows both manual control and automatic adjustment preferences.
- Q21 explains that task-priority collection is a separate follow-up.

## Live settings and behavior not established by this PDF

The form link previously opened a Google sign-in screen. Sign-in may be intentional for recruitment; it does not by itself prove email collection is enabled or disabled. Verify actual settings rather than infer anonymity or email storage from a login requirement.

Printed skip text is visible for Q1 No and Q2 No, directing respondents to Section 8. This suggests the intended routes are configured, but the PDF cannot prove their runtime behavior. It also cannot establish the end-of-section setting after Q21, semester validation, spreadsheet access, response editing, confirmation message, or respondent-summary visibility.

Use Preview to test:

1. Consent No → Exit without substantive questions.
2. Consent Yes + eligibility No → Exit without participant/profile questions.
3. Consent Yes + eligibility Yes → normal questions → completion, without showing the ineligible/declined exit message after Q21. The PDF printing all sections does not itself mean this route is broken.
4. Q5 rejects zero, negative/decimal values and A261, while accepting a whole semester number.
5. Optional Q10/Q18 can remain blank.
6. Other answers accept a useful duration unit; Not sure/It varies are retained as such, not converted to zero.
7. None/Not sure checkbox instructions are understood. Check contradictory selections during cleaning; do not assume automatic exclusivity.
8. Verify that email collection, access restrictions and response visibility match the participant information.

## Recommended next action

Correct Q8 and the section title, finish contact/participant-code arrangements, and run the three branching routes in Preview. Then use a small pilot with the supervisor's agreed process, record confusion and actual completion time, and revise before broader recruitment. Do not treat pilot size as sufficient model-training evidence.
